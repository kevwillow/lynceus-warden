Limits of this record

Read this section before relying on anything above. Every point here is a limit of the method, not a caveat added out of caution.

1. The record is keyed on MAC address

A device that rotates its address appears in this record as unrelated rows, and this document cannot tell you that they are the same device. This works for surveillance infrastructure, which broadcasts from a stable address because it is not trying to hide. It does not work for consumer trackers, and a reader who treats an absence here as proof that nothing was present is reading the method rather than the world.

2. Retention may have removed earlier sightings

Where a retention limit is in force, the first seen date in this document is the earliest RETAINED sighting and not necessarily the earliest observation.

3. A gap is not an absence

Sensor uptime is not recorded anywhere, so absence of data cannot be distinguished from absence of a device. A gap in this record may mean the device was elsewhere. It may equally mean nothing was listening: the sensor was off, the adapter had dropped, or the operator was not there. Reading a gap as "it stopped following me" is reading an artifact of the recording.

4. GPS is the receiver's position, not the device's

Coordinates recorded with an evidence snapshot are where the recording sensor was at that moment. They are not a fix on the observed device, which broadcasts no position. The two are close enough to be worth recording and they are not the same thing.

5. A watchlist match selects a device, it does not identify one

Any device named in this record is named because it matched a rule in the operator's watchlist. Those rules can cover a whole manufacturer prefix or an address range, so a match means the rule SELECTED this address. It does not mean the device has been individually verified as the equipment the rule describes, and an ordinary consumer device sharing a prefix will match in exactly the same way.

6. The hash does not prove the contents are true

The manifest in this bundle shows that these files are unchanged since the export was produced. It does not prove the observations are accurate, and it is not a third party attestation: the operator's own installation produced this bundle, and the operator controls the database it was produced from.
